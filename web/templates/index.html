What is the BAT-BMS Attack?

A Chinese smartphone app called BAT-BMS is being used to remotely disable e-rickshaws (tirris/totos) by connecting to their Battery Management System (BMS) via Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) and toggling the discharge switch, which cuts power to the motor.

Why Are E-Rickshaws Vulnerable?

No Authentication

Most budget BMS units accept Bluetooth connections without requiring any PIN, password, or pairing verification.

No Encryption

BLE communication is unencrypted. Commands including the discharge toggle are sent in plaintext.

Open Advertising

The BMS broadcasts its presence continuously, making it easy for anyone to find with a scanning app.

Identifiable Names

Devices broadcast names like "BMS" or "Xiaoxiang" that make them trivial to identify as targets.

How to Protect Your E-Rickshaw

1

Disconnect Bluetooth (Immediate)

Physically disconnect the Bluetooth module from your BMS. This is usually a small board or antenna inside the battery pack. Ask your mechanic or battery supplier for help. This completely eliminates the attack surface.

2

Shield the BMS (Short-term)

If you need Bluetooth for monitoring, wrap the BMS in metal shielding or use a metal enclosure to reduce the Bluetooth signal range. Even partial shielding reduces the distance from which an attacker can connect.

3

Upgrade Your BMS (Long-term)

Replace your BMS with one that supports PIN-based Bluetooth pairing and encrypted communication. Ask for "secure BLE" or "PIN-protected BMS" from your supplier.

4

Demand Better Standards (Advocacy)

Ask your battery and BMS supplier to provide firmware updates that add authentication. Report the issue to local transport authorities. Support industry standards for BMS security.
